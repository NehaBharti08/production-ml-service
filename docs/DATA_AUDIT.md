# Data Audit

> **NOT FOR CLINICAL USE.** This document reports an engineering data audit. Nothing in it is clinically validated or fit to inform patient care.

## Summary

The dataset survives the audit. It is genuinely hard, genuinely messy, and carries real time structure — which is what makes it worth operating a model on. Four findings changed the pipeline:
1. **A deterministic label leak.** 1,652 expired-discharge encounters with **0 positives**. Removed.
2. **The time proxy holds.** 5/8 independent signals shift monotonically across `encounter_id`, including a dated pharmacovigilance event. The split can honestly be called chronological.
3. **Right-censoring at the tail.** First-encounter positive rate collapses 56% in the final 5% of the ordering — labels are missing, not negative. A censoring buffer now discards it.
4. **`payer_code` is time-confounded**, not merely missing. Dropped.

## 4. Leakage hunt

### 4.1 Expired discharges — genuine leakage

`discharge_disposition_id` in [11, 19, 20, 21] means the patient died.

**1,652 such encounters contain 0 positive labels.** Not few — *zero*. A dead patient cannot be readmitted, so the label is deterministic and a model would learn `died → not readmitted` directly from the discharge code.

### 4.2 Hospice discharges — excluded, but not leakage

Codes [13, 14] are hospice. These are **not** deterministic: 43 of 771 are positive (5.58%), so hospice patients genuinely are readmitted.

They are excluded on clinical-relevance grounds — readmission is not a meaningful quality signal in end-of-life care — **not** as leakage. Conflating the two would overstate the finding.

### 4.3 Patient overlap across splits

101,766 encounters span 71,518 patients, so the same patient can land on both sides of any split. Keeping only each patient's first encounter removes 29,353 rows (99,343 → 69,990).

This costs the prior-utilisation richness of repeat visits, which is a real loss. It is accepted because the alternative — a held-out estimate inflated by memorised patient idiosyncrasy — is worse.

### 4.4 Columns dropped

| Column | Reason |
|:--|:--|
| `weight` | 96.86% missing. Imputing a value present in 3% of records would manufacture a variable rather than measure one. The honest move is to drop it and say so. |
| `examide` | Zero variance — constant 'No' across all 101,766 rows. |
| `citoglipton` | Zero variance — constant 'No' across all 101,766 rows. |
| `payer_code` | TIME-CONFOUNDED, not merely missing. Capture rises from 0% in the first encounter_id decile to 86% in the last, because the field was rolled out mid-period. Under a chronological split the model would learn 'payer_code present' as a proxy for 'later era' — an artifact of data capture with no clinical meaning, which cannot generalise. See docs/DECISIONS/0004-temporal-split-proxy.md. |

## 5. Temporal proxy verification

**This dataset has no timestamp column.** Not a missing one — none exists. The only time signal is the ordering of `encounter_id`. Claiming 'temporal validation' without evidence would be an unearned claim, so the proxy was tested before being trusted.

**Test:** clinical practice change is *directional* — a drug is adopted, a field starts being captured — whereas a meaningless row ordering produces non-monotonic noise. Spearman rank correlation across 10 deciles distinguishes them.

**Criteria:** |ρ| > 0.8 and p < 0.01, with at least 3 signals trending.

| Signal | First decile | Last decile | Δ | ρ | p | Trends |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|:--:|
| medical_specialty missing | 0.364 | 0.679 | +0.315 | +0.891 | 0.00054 | **yes** |
| payer_code missing | 1.000 | 0.138 | -0.862 | -0.875 | 0.00090 | **yes** |
| A1Cresult measured | 0.188 | 0.179 | -0.009 | +0.115 | 0.75142 | no |
| max_glu_serum measured | 0.089 | 0.011 | -0.077 | -0.685 | 0.02888 | no |
| insulin prescribed | 0.465 | 0.602 | +0.137 | +0.891 | 0.00054 | **yes** |
| metformin prescribed | 0.168 | 0.216 | +0.048 | +0.964 | 0.00001 | **yes** |
| pioglitazone prescribed | 0.037 | 0.080 | +0.043 | +0.903 | 0.00034 | **yes** |

**Result: 5/8 trending — PASSED.**

### 5.1 The strongest evidence: a dated external event

Trend tests show the ordering carries time signal. A *discontinuity* does something stronger — it dates the ordering against the real world, which is a prediction that could have failed.

Rosiglitazone prescribing holds steady, then drops **3.7 percentage points in a single bin** at the 80.0th percentile of the ordering — **7.7× larger** than any other bin-to-bin change.

Pioglitazone — the competing drug in the same class — is unaffected, so this is drug-specific, not a general recording change.

That is the Avandia collapse: Nissen & Wolski's NEJM meta-analysis (May 2007) linking rosiglitazone to myocardial infarction, followed by an FDA black-box warning that November. Prescribing halved and stayed down.

**The ordering reproduces a dated pharmacovigilance event.** That places the 80th percentile of `encounter_id` at roughly mid-2007 and makes the chronological claim about as well-evidenced as it can be without a date column.

## 6. Right-censoring at the end of the window

A first encounter is labelled positive only if a *subsequent* encounter exists in the data. Near the end of collection those later encounters are unobserved — so the label is **missing, not negative**.

The effect appears only after first-encounter deduplication: across all encounters the positive rate is flat over the period. After deduplication it sits at 9.54% through the first 80% and falls to 4.17% in the final 5% — a **56% relative collapse**.

**Mitigation:** the final 5% of the ordering is discarded as a censoring buffer before splitting. Without it that region lands entirely in the test set, depressing every held-out metric for a reason unrelated to the model — and Phase 6 drift monitoring would chase an artifact of data collection.

## 7. The split

**Claim supported by the evidence:** chronological split (encounter_id proxy, empirically verified)

| Split | Rows | Positive rate |
|:--|--:|--:|
| Train | 39,894 | 9.87% |
| Validation | 13,298 | 8.99% |
| Test | 13,298 | 7.56% |

Split by position in `encounter_id` order, never at random. A random split on time-structured data leaks the future into training: the model would see post-2007 prescribing patterns while being evaluated on them.

**A residual gradient remains** after the censoring buffer (9.87% → 7.56%). Part is real prior-probability shift; part is residual censoring the buffer does not fully remove. It is reported rather than smoothed away, and Phase 2 must not attribute it to model quality.

## 8. Separability sanity check

Deliberately inverted logic: an unconstrained decision tree is fitted *hoping it does badly*. On the widely-circulated synthetic symptom-to-disease datasets a tree like this reaches ~100% AUC, which reveals the data is trivially separable and worthless for demonstrating anything.

| | |
|:--|--:|
| Train ROC-AUC | 1.0 |
| **Test ROC-AUC** | **0.5185** |
| Tree depth | 47 |
| Leaves | 5,034 |
| Alarm threshold | 0.95 |
| Alarm triggered | no |

> An unconstrained tree memorises the training set (1.000 AUC) and generalises poorly (0.519 test AUC). This gap is the expected, healthy result: the problem is genuinely hard. A test AUC above 0.95 would indicate leakage or a synthetic dataset.

## 9. Baselines — the floor to beat

| Baseline | Accuracy | Recall | PR-AUC | Brier |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| majority_class | 0.9243 | 0.0000 | 0.0757 | 0.0699 |
| prevalence_constant | 0.9243 | 0.0000 | 0.0757 | 0.0699 |
| heuristic_number_inpatient | 0.8454 | 0.1491 | 0.0862 | 0.0750 |

**The accuracy trap, made concrete.** The majority-class baseline scores **92.43% accuracy with 0% recall** — it never identifies a single at-risk patient. Any project reporting accuracy on this task is reporting that number.

**The bar that matters** is the one-feature heuristic: PR-AUC 0.0862 (95% CI 0.0796–0.0959), available at admission with no model at all. A trained model must clear this with non-overlapping intervals to justify the cost of deploying, monitoring and retraining it.

## What this means for Phase 2

- Report **PR-AUC** as the headline against a 7.56% test prevalence floor. Never accuracy.
- Beat the one-feature heuristic with **non-overlapping confidence intervals**, or the model has not earned its infrastructure.
- Do not attribute the train→test positive-rate gradient to model quality; it is partly a property of the data.
- Report subgroup performance only where n ≥ 500, and report it even when unflattering.
- Expect modest numbers. The published ceiling is ROC-AUC 0.65–0.68. That is the point of this project, not a shortfall.
